Winston's emerging research portfolio

You do not need one dissertation topic today. You need a coherent intellectual territory. These questions already cluster around access, vulnerability, technology, intervention quality, and what happens when systems give people incomplete support.

Financial vulnerability + life insurance

How do financial-service and insurance systems operate in communities where intergenerational economic insecurity is persistent? What distinguishes protection from extraction?

Post-bariatric life after surgery

What happens after the procedure, especially when patients receive inconsistent nutrition, behavioral-health, or long-term support? Who is giving guidance, and what outcomes follow?

Digital mental health + Skybound

Can interactive narrative tools translate CBT concepts into accessible practice while protecting privacy, avoiding overclaiming, and improving engagement?

Program evaluation + community systems

Which services actually produce lasting changes in daily life, not just short-term program outputs? How can participant experience change what programs measure?

Research narrative that could hold the application together

I am interested in what happens when institutions offer people a solution, but the structure around that solution is incomplete.

That frame can legitimately connect several experiences without pretending they are already one dissertation: families purchasing financial protection while remaining economically vulnerable; bariatric patients receiving a life-changing intervention without consistent long-term guidance; digital mental-health tools that can widen access but require rigorous ethical evaluation; and community programs whose success should be judged by what people can actually carry into daily life after the program ends.

The common question: How can social work design, evaluate, and improve interventions so that access to a service becomes durable capability rather than a temporary transaction?
