Origin

WHERE THIS
STARTED

In July 2022, I flew to Tijuana and had a vertical sleeve gastrectomy. They removed about 80 percent of my stomach. I woke up different. Not fixed, not healed, not finished. Just different. That was the beginning.

What most people want is the before-and-after photo. The dramatic number. The transformation reel. And yeah, the physical change was real. The weight came off. My joints stopped hurting. I could cross my legs. I could fit a seatbelt without an extension. Things I'd stopped noticing I was missing suddenly came back.

"But nobody talks about what happens to your identity when your body changes that fast. Nobody prepares you for the version of yourself that shows up after."

I started creating content because I couldn't find what I actually needed online. Everyone was selling transformation. I was trying to understand it. There's a difference.

Then I went back to school. Not because I had it all figured out. Because I wanted to build the kind of credibility that could sit next to my lived experience and make both of them stronger. The social work path and the platform aren't separate. They're the same thing.

I'm not presenting a finished version of myself here. I'm building in public, on purpose. Because that's what's real.

GetOutMyBelly

IT'S NOT
JUST WEIGHT

The name means something different depending on how deep you're willing to go. Here's what it actually is, layer by layer.

01

The Surface: Physical Transformation

Weight loss surgery. The scale going down. Fitting into clothes you couldn't wear before. Looking different in the mirror. This part is real and it matters. It's also the easiest part for people to understand, which is why it's usually the only part they talk about.

02

One Level Down: Getting Rid of What Doesn't Belong

Removing the habits, the coping mechanisms, the avoidance strategies. The things you used to feel okay when you didn't actually feel okay. Surgery removes the stomach. It doesn't remove the reason you used food the way you did. That part you have to do yourself.

03

Deeper: Confronting the Real Stuff

Trauma. Identity. The way you were treated when you were bigger and the way you're treated now. The disorienting experience of pretty privilege after years of being overlooked. The psychological weight that doesn't show up on any scale. This is where most people's transformation stories stop. This is where mine gets interesting.

04

The Bottom: Becoming Intentional

Deciding, on purpose, who you are and how you show up. Not reacting. Not surviving. Not performing for people who didn't see you before. GetOutMyBelly is ultimately about getting intentional about the life you're actually trying to live, and doing the work to get there.

The Platform

GET OUT
MY BELLY

This started as a way to document what nobody else was being honest about. Surgery is one decision. Everything after it is thousands of decisions.

I talk about the things the wellness industry doesn't want to touch. Transfer addiction. The way people treat you differently when you look different. The gap between how you look and how you actually feel inside. The criticism from people who never struggled with their weight telling you that surgery is the "easy way."

I also bring in the social work lens now. Behavior change, systems, access, the psychological side of transformation. The platform is evolving on purpose.

Advocacy

WHAT I'M
FIGHTING FOR

Mental Health Access

The people who need mental health care most are the ones with the least access to it. Insurance barriers, provider shortages, rural deserts, cultural stigma. This is a structural problem and it needs structural solutions.

Substance Use and Trauma

I've had people tell me surgery creates transfer addiction. Some of them were right to raise it. Most of them had never lived any version of what they were criticizing. Addiction is not a moral failure. It is what happens when pain has nowhere else to go. Treatment has to start there.

Expungement and Justice Reform

A record should not be a permanent sentence. People change. People grow. The system's inability to account for that is a policy choice, not an inevitability.

System Navigation Barriers

The systems that are supposed to help people are often designed in ways that make them nearly impossible to use. That's not an accident. Advocacy means making that visible and pushing for it to change.

Fathers and Families

Fathers, especially Black fathers navigating poverty or system involvement, are consistently written out of the support conversation. I'm building toward changing that in Southern Illinois specifically.

Why This Is Personal

I'm not advocating from the outside. Every issue on this list connects to something I've lived, someone I've lost, or a moment where the system should have shown up and didn't. That's not a liability in this work. That's the most important qualification I have.

I also hold a BSW and I'm entering an MSW program. I'm doing the reading, running the research, sitting in the policy rooms. Both things are true at the same time. That's the whole point.

SIMRC — A Community Space That Actually Meets People Where They Are

Southern Illinois has significant gaps in mental health resources, especially for men who wouldn't necessarily walk into a traditional therapy setting. SIMRC is being built as a peer-centered space for connection, mental health navigation, reentry support, and skill development.

This is not a crisis center or a last resort. It's a place where men can show up before things get that far. The kind of place that didn't exist when it would have been useful to me.

🏠
Housing Initiative

Father-Centered Stabilization Housing

Fathers in recovery, reentry, or navigating custody systems often have no housing option that accounts for their actual situation. This initiative is exploring transitional and stabilization housing models designed specifically around that reality.

Values

WHAT I
ACTUALLY BELIEVE

Not a mission statement. Not branding copy. Things I actually think, shaped by things I've actually been through.

Trauma Precedes Behavior

Before asking why someone does something, you have to ask what happened to them. That changes every intervention, every conversation, every policy position.

"Understanding before judgment. Every time."

Lived Experience Is Expertise

I know things about navigating systems that I couldn't have learned in a classroom. That knowledge belongs in the rooms where decisions get made, not just in the waiting room.

"A degree tells you I studied it. My story tells you I survived it."

Truth Over Perfection

I'm not interested in presenting a finished version of myself. I'm building in public, figuring things out in real time, and being honest about what I don't know yet. That's more useful than a polished brand.

"Perfect is a lie. Real is what connects."

Credentials and Credibility Are Not the Same

I went back to school partly because I want both. The degree and the story. The research and the receipts. Neither one is enough by itself.

"The letters after your name mean more when something came before them."

Access Is Not Equal and That Is a Choice

The systems that exist, exist because people decided they should. That means they can be changed. That's not optimism. That's just how policy works.

"It's not broken. It's working for someone else."

Transformation Is Not Linear

It is not a before-and-after. It's not a moment. It's a thousand small decisions made in the absence of certainty. The people who've done it know what I mean.

"You don't arrive. You just keep choosing."
