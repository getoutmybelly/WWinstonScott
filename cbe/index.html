The CBE Principle

About the philosophy

Continuance-Based Eating (CBE) begins with a simple ethical intuition: my nourishment does not have to depend on intentionally ending another organism’s life when that organism can continue living. CBE looks first at the relationship between harvest and continuance. Fruit can be taken while a tree or vine keeps living. Leaves and herbs can often be gathered without killing the plant. Annual crops can be eaten after they naturally complete their life cycle, with enough viable seed retained so the next generation can continue.

CBE does not require every seed, nut, fruit, or potential offspring to become a new organism. Its boundary is between ending an existing life and using part of reproductive abundance while preserving continuation. A seed that might have fallen to the ground is potential future life; eating some of those seeds is different, within CBE, from intentionally killing the living organism that produced them.

The philosophy is practical rather than perfectionistic. Agriculture occurs inside ecosystems, so eating cannot guarantee that no organism is ever affected. The narrower aim is to avoid making the intentional ending of a living food-producing organism the mechanism of nourishment when a continuance-preserving alternative exists.

Nourish life. Do not intentionally end life for food when continuance is possible. Preserve the line.

The four-question check

1. Does the producer survive?If the organism keeps living after harvest, that is the clearest CBE fit.
2. Is it an annual at natural completion?An annual can fit when it has naturally matured or senesced before harvest.
3. Is continuation preserved?Seeds, nuts, grains and pulses can be eaten while enough viable material remains for the next generation.
4. Does harvest itself end the organism?If normal harvest intentionally kills a still-living organism, it falls outside the clearest CBE boundary or becomes method-dependent.

How CBE differs from other labels

Veganism focuses primarily on avoiding animal exploitation and animal-derived products. Fruitarianism usually centers heavily or exclusively on fruit and sometimes seeds or nuts. CBE uses a different decision rule: continuance. The question is what obtaining the food does to the organism producing it.

Animal-derived foods are intentionally left unclassified in this version. A simple “no death required” test does not by itself resolve questions about animal welfare, exploitation, breeding or production practices.

Why CBE Includes Seeds & Nuts

Potential life is not the same as ending an existing life

A seed can become another plant under the right conditions, but CBE does not treat eating a seed as automatically equivalent to killing the living parent plant. The distinction is between ending a life already underway and intercepting one of many possible future organisms.

Continuation is the boundary

The goal is not to make every seed germinate. The goal is to keep the reproductive line intact by retaining enough viable seed or planting material for continuation.

Eat some. Save some. Continue the line.

Examples

  • Beans and lentils: mature seeds are food, while a portion can be retained for future planting.
  • Wheat, oats, corn and quinoa: annual crops can be gathered at maturity with some grain retained as seed.
  • Almonds and walnuts: taking nuts does not kill the mature tree.
  • Sunflower and pumpkin seeds: some viable seed can be saved while the remainder is used as food.

Why some foods are method-dependent

Biology does not always give a neat yes/no answer. Potatoes are living tubers that can create clones; garlic cloves are propagation material; banana fruiting stalks naturally die after fruiting while the underground clump continues. CBE therefore asks what actually happens during harvest rather than classifying a food by name alone.

CBE is a philosophical food framework, not medical advice. A nutritionally complete diet still requires attention to protein, vitamin B12, iron, calcium, iodine, zinc, vitamin D, omega-3 fats and overall energy intake.
